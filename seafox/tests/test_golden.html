design_spec § 20.5, the last and most expensive oracle. The game is deterministic from cold boot until the first input, so a rendered frame at a fixed tick is reproducible to the byte. Two halves, and they are different kinds of check: anchors compare the frame against the specification — the six-colour palette, the waterline row, the HUD row — and would catch a renderer that has been consistently wrong since it was written; goldens compare it against this port's own past, and can only tell you it changed. A golden failure is a question: ask what changed and whether it was meant, and never regenerate to silence a red page — that promotes a bug to the reference.

Two runs are captured. The demo run is the cold boot, deterministic because nothing touches it. The scripted run presses a fixed sequence of keys and so reaches everything the demo cannot — the launch, a played round, a loss, the outro, the drain and the fresh-submarine refill: 15 rounds across 4 games. Every bug the Chapter 9–20 audit found lived in that code, and the demo goldens stayed green through all of them. Its phase timeline is the most diagnostic thing on this page — a shifted transition names the tick a round started, ended or drained differently, which a changed hash alone never would. To regenerate deliberately, see the headers of golden_frames.js and golden_play.js.

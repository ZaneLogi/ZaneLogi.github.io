Lunar Lander — the crash explosion BOOM

A faithful port of the original BOOM routine (A34573.1A) and its debris pictures (A34599.1C). An explosion is 7 pieces — a tumbling cabin octagon plus 6 debris fragments — animated over a step counter INDEX that runs 1 → 127. Each piece flies straight out from the impact point at its own constant velocity (offset = delta × INDEX), and winks out at its own staggered time; the octagon lasts longest (the "semi-intact cabin while the struts scatter") and tumbles through its 8 poses by INDEX&7.

The "random debris" is a random 1-of-4 pattern, not a random subset of glyphs: RNDOM (0–3, from a free-running counter) picks one of four hand-authored patterns, each a fixed set of 6 fragment glyphs + 7 velocities (the 12 glyphs live at $4F1C–$4FBE). Positioning is a single cumulative beam walk — the CPU emits dark relative moves (VGVCTR, brightness 0) between pieces. Full decode in docs/research_explosion.md. The lander rests at the centre, so the residual-velocity drift (DELX/DELY) is zero here.

Loops: intact lander → explosion → reposition → replay with a new random pattern. Scrub pauses and steps through any INDEX frame to inspect the burst; zoom magnifies the (deliberately small, arcade-faithful) fragments. Red dot = the impact origin (0,0).
